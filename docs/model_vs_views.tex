\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The draft presents the model as an account of visual stability and anticipatory
remapping (Figure~1 of the draft). This note compares it with the three views
organised in \code{docs/visual_stability_review.tex}: spatiotopic, retinotopic
remapping and deflationary. It reads what the model implements from the code, sets
what the draft claims against what the code does, and collects evidence for and
against the model from the project's own results and from the literature. The
model is closest to a contingency-based account of stability, and only partly
an account of remapping: its anticipation is an address on a lattice, not a shift
of receptive fields, and the anticipated effect is not read out at run time.
Stability across starting positions has not been tested. In a two-spot remapping
paradigm run on the three standard-window runs, with the attentional filter first on the
fixation spot and then on the target, the conditions face of the recruited location responds
at the target before the shift and, in two of three runs, the effects face responds, selectively,
at the place where the first spot will be after the shift; the effects field of a location also
follows the landing of a single stimulus as a trained pairing (supporting analysis), and the
maps show indirect retinotopy in a black-box probe.
\end{abstract}

The figure shows a column of units, one per lattice position, that spans three
layers: a condition (the foveal view before the saccade), an effect (the view after
it) and an attentional filter that selects the saccade. The draft reads these as LIP
and FEF populations: LIP$_\mathrm{c}$ holds the condition, LIP$_\mathrm{e}$ the
anticipated effect, and FEF the attentional filter that drives the saccade. The
retinal view reaches the condition layer; within one column the same unit pattern
addresses the effect and the saccade. This is the draft's account of both
phenomena: \emph{stability} is the congruence of the anticipated effect with the
actual one, and \emph{anticipatory remapping} is that the effect layer is
activated by the condition before the saccade.

\section{Anticipatory remapping in the model: what is implemented}

Three facts from the code bound the claim.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{The anticipation is an address.} The goal cell addresses a condition
    prototype, an attention prototype (the saccade) and an effect prototype (the
    expected post-saccadic view) at once. The effect prototype is the stored
    expectation, a lookup, not a computed prediction of the image.
  \item \textbf{The expected effect is not read out at run time.} The effects map is
    used only in the offline update and in the match that defines competence
    (\code{offline_controller.py} l.482, l.510, l.547). At decision time the model
    reads the conditions winner and the attention prototype at that cell
    (\code{generate_saccade}, l.291; \code{get_action_from_condition}, l.671);
    \code{test.py} never calls the effects map. So the congruence
    of the draft is computed during training, not as a comparison at run time.
  \item \textbf{No receptive field moves.} Maps are fixed during a saccade and change
    only in the epoch update (l.369). The model does not shift receptive fields
    toward the saccade target and has no pre-saccadic response at a future field,
    the signatures reported in LIP and FEF (Duhamel et al., 1992; Zirnsak et al.,
    2014). The draft's neural-substrates stub lists remapping of FEF and LIP
    receptive fields, which the model does not reproduce, as its abstract now states.
\end{enumerate}

In the draft's terms the model therefore gives the \emph{functional} counterpart
of remapping, a learned relation between a view, a saccade and its effect, and not
its neural signatures. Whether that relation could produce the signatures is
open (Section~\ref{sec:tests}).

\subsection{Against spatiotopy}

\emph{Agreement.} None on mechanism. The model has no world-centred map, no
eye-position signal (absolute eye position stays inside the environment), and
needs neither for its behaviour. It agrees only in the observation that
spatiotopic neurons are a minority and that eye-position signals are unreliable
after a saccade (Xu et al., 2012; Morris et al., 2012), which removes a motive for
a global frame.

\emph{Disagreement.} The model is silent on the spatiotopic evidence: the positional
motion aftereffect (Turi and Burr, 2012), the fMRI repetition suppression across a
saccade (Fairhall et al., 2017) and spatiotopic updating within 150~ms (Fabius et
al., 2019). These tests oppose the frames by a saccade; the model was never run on
such a paradigm. Either it must reproduce the spatiotopic pattern from address
lookups, or it predicts the effects away.

\subsection{Against retinotopic remapping}

\emph{Agreement.} The model uses an action-derived signal (the anchor) and an
anticipated effect, and it names FEF and LIP, the areas where remapping is
reported. It also keeps retina-centred inputs and actions. The draft's abstract
takes the retinotopic hypothesis as its starting point (l.64).

\emph{Disagreement.} Remapping accounts shift activity or pointers to the
post-saccadic location before the eye lands, and need a signal that says where each
item will land. The model has neither: no receptive field moves, the effect is a
learned lookup, and nothing computes a landing position. The anchor resembles a
corollary discharge, but it addresses a lattice cell, not a retinal displacement.
The draft therefore starts from the retinotopic hypothesis ``without shifting
receptive fields'' (l.64), which agrees with the topological reading in
\code{docs/topological_stability.tex}.

\subsection{Against the deflationary view}

\emph{Agreement.} This is the closest view. The model stores condition--action--effect
triples, which is the learned relation between movement and sensory change that the
sensorimotor account calls mastery of contingencies (O'Regan and No\"e, 2001), it
has no predictive model of space, and its stability needs no feedback correction.
The failures found, in which the effect is not the effect of the addressed saccade
(reversals), are the failures a contingency account predicts.

\emph{Disagreement.} The sensorimotor account denies internal representations of
the scene, whereas the model stores explicit prototype maps and a goal cell. It also
does not use post-saccadic landmarks or the suppression of displacement
(Bridgeman et al., 1975; Deubel et al., 1996), and it does not explain why displacements
are undetected during saccades.

\section{Tests and results}
\label{sec:results}

Tests run on the trained maps since the first version of this note. Scripts are listed
in \code{SCRIPTS.md}; data are in \code{tests/} (folders named below).

\paragraph{Start-offset invariance (E1a, pilot).} \code{scripts/exp_start_offsets.py}.
The scanpath's invariance to retina start offsets was 0.78 for the model against 0.91
for a salience-only control; the pre-stated rule failed. Stability across starting
positions is therefore not shown.

\paragraph{Forward relation between paired fields (E3, pilot).}
\code{scripts/exp_predictive_field.py}. Per lattice cell, the conditions-unit and
effects-unit fields are compared with the saccade vector of the attention prototype.
The forward-direction cosine beat its shuffled null in 4 of 5 runs with the mean
readout and in 2 of 5 with peak readouts, and the shift magnitude was near zero
(slope 0.01 to 0.08). Superseded by the paradigm below.

\paragraph{Retinotopy of the maps (Phase 1).} Reported in
\code{docs/topological_stability.tex}. In three runs of the standard configuration
(\code{champion.md}: 64 px window) the black-box spot probe gives
$z>3$ in 6 of 6 maps (median $\rho$ 0.83 for conditions, 0.77 for effects, median $z$ 48.5
and 41.9) and exceeds a shuffled-pixel-layout control in 6 of 6; the
phase-encoded probe gives $z>3$ in 6 of 6 (median $\rho$ 0.75 and 0.71). The maps are trained
on view content only: the retinotopy is indirect.

\paragraph{Two-spot remapping paradigm (Phase 2).}
\code{scripts/exp_two_spot_paradigm.py}, \code{scripts/fig_two_spot.py}; plan in \code{predictive_remapping.md}, Section 2.7;
main text in \code{draft.tex}, Section 4.3.3.

\emph{The classic experiments.} A neuron has a receptive field at a fixed retinal place $c$; for a saccade of vector $s$,
a stimulus at retinal place $x$ is at $x-s$ afterwards, so the place that will fall on the field is $c+s$ (the future field).
In monkey LIP the field is mapped in fixation, then a stimulus is flashed just before a saccade at the place the saccade will
bring into the field; the neuron responds although the stimulus is outside the field at that moment, and not in a fixation
control (Duhamel et al., 1992; Colby and Goldberg, 1999; the control and the trace response are from the draft's review, the
original paper is not in the repository). Neupane et al. (2016, in the repository, methods read) map V4 with probes at random
places of a $10\times10$ grid over $40^\circ$ while monkeys make horizontal saccades from a fixation point to a lit target (towards
or away from the field): P1 in fixation, P2 just before the saccade, P3 after it; the field centre is the centre of mass of the
responses at about 75 ms (current field) and at 150 to 300 ms (shifted), and a shift within $20^\circ$ of the saccade
direction is future-field (FF) remapping, a shift to the target saccade-target (ST) remapping. The tests are the saccade
vector set relative to the field, the probe flashed before the saccade, and the fixation control and time course.

\emph{What the simulation shares.} The first spot $F$ is the fixation point, the second spot $T$ the target, $v=T-F$ the saccade
vector, a lone probe on a grid the probe grid, the receptive field of a face of the recruited location the receptive field of
the neuron, and a statistic against shuffled saccade vectors the comparison with chance. Before the saccade $F$ is at the centre
of the retina and $T$ at $v$; after it $T$ is at the centre and $F$ at $-v$, each stimulus displaced by $-v$.

\emph{Paradigm.} Three trained runs with the $64\times64$ window (\code{tests/phase_fixed_pilot_2026-10-10}), no further
learning, 300 displays per run. $F$ and $T$ are red Gaussian blobs (sigma 3 px) on the white retina through the Gabor front end;
$T$ is drawn from the mean of the conditions prototypes read as a map over the fovea (offsets under 16 px redrawn). The view with
both spots recruits a location: the nearest conditions prototype wins and a Gaussian on the lattice spreads its activity. A lone
probe is flashed at the places of a grid over the retina (4 px steps); it is won by one unit of a map and the recruited location
responds as far as that unit lies in its lattice neighbourhood, so the probe drives it only inside the receptive field of the face
read. Probes more than 28 px from the gaze are left out (a spot near the window edge leaves a blank view won by one fixed unit).
The response at a place is its percentile rank among the probed places of the map (1 strongest, 0.5 a random place).

\emph{Narrative with the attentional filter.} The saccade is chosen by a Gaussian attentional filter that multiplies the salience
map (Section 4.1.2 of the draft). Before the saccade the filter is on $F$: the view then, with $F$ at the centre and $T$ at $v$,
is what the conditions prototype of the location holds. The saccade moves the filter to $T$: $T$ is at the centre and $F$ at $-v$,
the view the effects prototype holds, and the effects face is pre-activated because the filter has not yet moved. Expected: the
conditions face responds at $T$ before the shift ($v$) and the effects face at $F$ after it ($-v$); the conditions face at $-v$ and
the effects face at $v$ are specificity checks; the place 0 is shared by all prototypes (all hold $F$ before and $T$ after at the
centre), so under the winner read-out it is not selective and has no criterion.

\emph{Statistics.} Mean percentile over the testable displays (both maps defined, $|v|\le28$ px per axis) with a 95\% bootstrap
interval over the recruited cells (displays share cells); for the places that depend on $v$, chance is the same mean with the
saccade vectors shuffled across displays (500 shuffles), $p$ = share of shuffles at least as high ($p=0.002$ is the floor).
The statistics were defined after earlier ones failed (post hoc). Readout check on hand-built maps: fields recovered within 1 px;
with spots planted at 0, $v$ and $-v$ the percentile at the planted place is 0.99 against 0.72 for shuffled vectors (criterion:
at least 0.9 and above the shuffled mean with $p\le0.02$; the first criterion, shuffled mean at most 0.6, failed because tied
near-zero responses raise every percentile, and was replaced).

\emph{Result.} The two spots recruit 18 to 23 of the 100 locations per run, and the recruited location holds a saccade 13 to
15 px from the displayed one, so recruitment by the two-blob view is rough. The conditions face responds at $T$ before the shift
in all runs (0.88 to 0.91, $p=0.002$), the least informative result because recruitment uses a view that contains $T$. The
effects face responds at $F$ after the shift in two runs (0.69 and 0.81 against 0.51 and 0.65, $p=0.002$), with the
dissociation expected (conditions face low at $-v$, 0.38 and 0.28; effects face low at $v$, 0.35 and 0.31) and a clear
contrast at $-v$ (0.31 and 0.53); in the third run the effects face is above 0.5 at $-v$ (0.61) but not above its shuffled value
(0.60), so the anticipation is not selective. The classic relation fails: the displacement of the effects field from the
conditions field was no closer to $-v$ than chance (error 21 to 23 px against 22 to 25 px), and classifying displays by
the nearest of future field, saccade target and current field gave a future-field share of 0.00 to 0.13 in earlier versions of
the readout. The field centres agree with the ranks (conditions centre 7 to 9 px from $T$ against 22 to 27 px for chance; effects
centre 11 to 19 px from the new place of $F$ in two runs against 25 to 32 px). Using the saccade stored in the recruited cell
instead of $v$, the partial correlation of the effects field with the landing of the conditions field is 0.32, 0.10 and
0.00 and the shift fraction 0.25, 0.45 and 0.36, within the permutation null.

\emph{Differences from the classic experiments.} No receptive field shifts: the maps are fixed during the saccade and the two
fields compared belong to two maps, not to two states of one neuron. There is no time axis, hence no probe just before the
saccade, no fixation control, no latency or time course, and the classic test (a response at $c+s$ absent in fixation and
appearing over time) cannot be run. The unit is not fixed: the displayed spots select the location and the relation to the saccade
is tested afterwards. Recruitment by two blobs is rough; the stimuli are a colour spot on white (a white spot on black gives a
blank fovea in this colour-opponent front end); the probe is a static lone spot read by a winner-based map; three runs with
displays sharing 18 to 23 locations, against tens to hundreds of independent neurons. The result is not predictive remapping in
the physiological sense.

\emph{Link to the hypothesis.} A location of the lattice is one location with a condition, an action and an effect face
learned from paired saccades. The remapping-like behaviour is a consequence of the pairing, not of a moving field: the effects
face holds the expected view after the saccade, including the new place of the stimulus that holds attention, and since the
location is recruited by the view before the saccade, that view is available in advance. A shift of a field would be the
physiological reading of a fixed pairing read at different times. The simulation supports the pairing in two of three runs;
the supporting analysis below tests it with single spots and a control trained with shuffled pairs. A time axis (the two faces
weighted over time, a fixation control, FF and ST classification as in V4) would turn the analogy into a test of a shifting
field. Earlier versions of the paradigm (direct recruitment, winner-take-all recruitment on the whole retina, a
baseline-subtracted probe, an effects readout shifted by $-v$) gave no future-field response and had read-out artefacts (blank views
outside the window); they are recorded in \code{predictive_remapping.md}.

\paragraph{Earlier single-spot paradigm and the learned pairing (supporting analysis).} The analyses
below were run first, with single spots; they test the pairing hypothesis (the effects face of a location is tuned to the
landing of the stimulus that activates its condition) and are the supporting analysis of \code{draft.tex}.
\code{scripts/exp_remap_feasibility.py} and \code{scripts/exp_remapping_paradigm.py};
rules fixed in \code{predictive_remapping.md}. For each cell $j$ the post-saccadic
field (CRF) is the effects-unit field, the pre-saccadic field is the response of
column $j$ through the model's read-out path (a lattice Gaussian around the conditions
winner), and the forward-predicted future field is $\mathrm{FF}_j=\mathrm{CRF}_j+d(j)$,
with $d(j)$ the saccade vector of the attention prototype. Only cells with FF inside the
fovea window are testable. The only quantitative benchmark is Neupane et al. (2016): the
share of future-field (FF) type remapping across V4 conditions is 0.27 to 0.82. The
latency and trace figures of LIP, V3A and FEF cannot be expressed at map level (no time
axis) and are not compared.

\emph{Feasibility.} In the three runs (\code{tests/phase_fixed_pilot_2026-10-10}) the
testable fraction is 0.91 to 1.00 (rule: at least 0.30) and competence over the last 50 epochs
is 0.76 to 0.89.

\emph{Result.} The FF-type share (median 0.74) lies inside the
Neupane span, but it exceeds the shuffled-pairing control (0.69) by 0.03 to 0.08, less than the spread
among seeds (FF-type 0.48 to 0.77), and the P1 share (median 0.55) does not exceed its control (0.62).
Untrained maps give 0.03 to 0.18. Condition C1 (inside the span and above every
control by more than the seed spread) fails. The toward/away asymmetry (C2) is not
supported: there are fewer than five toward cells in two of three runs, and the one run with
more (seed 022360, 14 cells) has FF-type 0.14 for toward against 0.55 for away,
with 0.41 and 0.48 in its shuffled control. The FF-type share is partly
geometric: with saccade vectors of 6.5 to 8 fovea pixels against a window half-width of 7.5
only cells whose field lies opposite the saccade are testable, which aligns the remapping
vector with it, and the shuffled control absorbs this. The trace response (P2) holds by
construction and is not evidence. The synthetic check passes (aligned pair P1 0.93, FF
0.92; shuffled pairing P1 0.11).

\emph{Post hoc: the pairing itself.} The two metrics above are computed on testable
cells, a selection that already aligns the remapping vector with the saccade, so they separate
the trained pairing from its control poorly. A direct statistic
(\code{scripts/exp_pairing_regression.py}, no rule fixed beforehand) uses all cells: the cosine
between $\mathrm{CRF}_{\mathrm{cond}}-\mathrm{CRF}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and $d$ is 0.75 to 0.97
in the three runs. But $d$ is already predicted
by the conditions field alone: the saccade goes to where the conditions unit's field lies, so
a forward cosine by itself is not evidence of a paired shift. Regressing
$d\approx a\,\mathrm{CRF}_{\mathrm{cond}}+b\,\mathrm{CRF}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (forward remapping predicts
$-b/a=1$), the peak-readout ratio $-b/a$ is 0.26, 0.46 and 0.28 in the three seeds
(median 0.28): the effects field is the conditions field shifted by
0.3 to 0.5 of the saccade. Field centres are compressed (mean-readout slopes below 1), so $-b/a$
is a lower bound.

\emph{Stimulus-centred landing and shuffled-pairing retrain } Take a lattice cell as one
location with conditions, effects and action faces. A stimulus at $p$ activates the conditions
winner $j$, the saccade lands it at $q=p-d(j)$, and $\mathrm{CRF}_{\mathrm{eff}}(j)$ is where the
location expects it. \code{scripts/exp_stimulus_landing.py} correlates the two after removing the
part of each linear in $p$, over all testable places, against a permutation of the effects units.
The partial correlation is 0.72, 0.84 and 0.55 in the three seeds
($p=0.003$ in all). The effects field thus follows the landing
beyond the stimulus's own place, partially (gaps of 3 to 4 px remain). Retraining
with the effects states permuted across the batch (\code{shuffle\_effects}, so a
location's conditions and effects no longer come from the same view) gives
$-0.71$, $-0.05$ and 0.29, and the median $-b/a$ falls from 0.28 to 0.01. In all three seeds
$r$ is significant with $r\ge0.2$ when trained normally and lower after shuffling (median $-0.05$
against 0.72), but in one (seed 022360) part of it remains, which is unexplained.

\emph{Limits.} Three runs; the toward/away split is a geometric stand-in for
hemifield, a deviation from Neupane; receptive-field centres from the soft readout are
compressed toward the mean; no latency, time course or response magnitude is tested. The landing
relation shows that the pairing in training produces an effects face at the landing of the
activating stimulus; it does not show that the nervous system does this, and a two-field cell
cannot be separated from a shifting field without the time course. Under the pre-stated
paradigm the model does not reproduce predictive remapping at map level beyond a shuffled-pairing control.

\section{What would settle it}
\label{sec:tests}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Run the stable-perception test.} Foveate a rewarded part of the object
    from varied starting views (\code{draft.tex}, l.115). Without it the stability
    claim is not tested.
  \item \textbf{Read out the expected effect at run time.} Compare the effects-map
    prototype at the goal cell with the actual post-saccadic fovea and use the
    mismatch; compare with and without it, to see whether anticipation changes
    behaviour.
  \item \textbf{Look for remapping signatures in the model.} Done at map level
    (Section~\ref{sec:results}) with a two-spot paradigm: the effects face responds, selectively, at the new place of the
    first spot in two of three runs, and the classic relation is not found. Still open: a version with a time axis (latency before
    saccade onset), and more runs.
  \item \textbf{Oppose the frames.} Simulate the spatiotopic paradigms (a saccade
    separating the retinal and world positions of a stimulus) and compare the
    model's behaviour with the human data.
  \item \textbf{Run a displacement paradigm.} Shift the target during the saccade and
    check whether the model detects it, against the suppression-of-displacement
    findings.
  \item \textbf{Ablate the pieces.} Remove the anchor, the effects map or the
    neighbourhood width, and measure stability and scanpath quality.
\end{enumerate}

The model supports a contingency-style account of stability (the pairing of a view,
a saccade and its effect on one lattice), with indirect evidence from the sweeps and
from the probe. It does not yet support the draft's claim as an account of
anticipatory remapping: the anticipation is an address, the expected effect is not
read out, no remapping signature beyond a control is reproduced at map level, and stability across starting
positions is untested.
